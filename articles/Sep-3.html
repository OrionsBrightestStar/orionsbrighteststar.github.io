2. Homotopy II

We now consider the notion of a strong deformation retract, which is sort of like a subset of a topological space that the topological space can morph, or retract, into. There are some more specific conditions that we wish to impose, stated formally below.

Definition 1 (Strong Deformation Retract)

A subspace $A$ of a topological space $X$ is known to be a strong deformation retract of $X$ if there exists a homotopy $F: X \times I \to X$, which we call a deformation, such that $$F(x,0)=x$$ $$F(x,1) \in A$$ and $$F(a,t)=a$$ for all $a \in A$ and all $t \in I$. If $F(a,t)=a$ only necessarily for $t = 1$, then $A$ is known as a deformation retract instead.

Consider the mapping cylinder of a function $f: X \to Y$, which is the topological space $$M_f = Y \cup_{f_0} X \times I$$

where we have that $f_0(x,0)=f(x).$ In other words, it is the union of the cylinder $X\times I$ and $Y$ stitched together in such a way that one of the edges of $X \times I$ is morphed into the image of $f(X)$ in $Y$. The map $r: M_f \to Y$ which maps each $(x,t)$ to $f_0(x,0)$ and each $y \in Y$ to itself is an example of a strong deformation retraction.
