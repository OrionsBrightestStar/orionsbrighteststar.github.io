2. Homotopy III and Topological Groups

Based on the Reparametrization Lemma from yesterday's blog entry, we obtain the following proposition:

Proposition 1

We necessarily have that $F * C \simeq F \text{ rel } X \times \partial I$, and we also have $C *F \simeq F \text{ rel }X \times \partial I$

Proof.

Using the Reparametrization Lemma, for the first case, let $\phi_1(t)=2t$ when $t\leq 1/2$ and 1 when $t \geq 1/2$, and $\phi_2(t)=t$, and for the second case, let $\phi_1(t)=0$ when $t\leq 1/2$, and $2t-1$ when $t\geq 1/2$, and then $\phi_2(t)=t$.

We can also define an "inverse homotopy," which essentially reverses the homotopy in question, like so:

Definition 1

Suppose we have a homotopy $F: X \times I \to Y$. Define $F^{-1}:X \times I \to Y$ where $F^{-1}(x,t)=F(x,1-t)$.

□
Proposition 2

If $F$ is a homotopy, then $F * F^{-1} \simeq C \text{ rel }X \times \partial I$ where $C(x,t)=F(x,0)$ for all $x$ and $t$.

Proof.

Let $\phi_1(t)=2t$ for $t\leq 1/2$, $\phi_1(t)=2-2t$ for $t\geq 1/2$, and $\phi_2(t)=0$ for all $t$. Then apply the Reparametrization Lemma. It follows that the two homotopies are homotopy equivalent.

3. Topological Groups

The notion of a topological group is profoundly important in the fields of topology and geometry. We will now begin to look at these mathematical objects.

Definition 2 (Topological Group)

A topological group is a Hausdorff topological space $G$ with a group structure on $G$ such that we have the following:

  1. Group multiplication $(g,h)\mapsto gh$ for $G\times G \to G$ is continuous.
  2. Group inversion $g \mapsto g^{-1}$ for $G\to G$ is continuous.
